# 5-minute Loom walkthrough — natural client presentation

## 0:00–0:35 — Opening
“Hi Maya, thanks for taking a look at the first draft of your new website. I’ve kept the familiar structure of the original site, but I’ve redesigned the experience to feel calmer, more personal, and easier to navigate.

I’ll quickly show you the desktop version first, then the mobile layout, and explain how I used the information from your profile.”

## 0:35–1:20 — Homepage / hero
“This is the first thing a new client sees. I wanted the opening message to answer three questions quickly: who the practice is for, where sessions are available, and what someone can expect emotionally.

The headline, ‘A quieter place to understand what you’re carrying,’ is intentionally human rather than overly clinical. The two main actions are also simple: schedule a consultation or learn more about Maya.”

## 1:20–2:05 — About
“Moving down, there’s a short personal introduction from Maya. Instead of a long professional biography at the top, I focused on how therapy feels and how Maya works with clients.

The language is collaborative and reassuring. It tells visitors that they don’t need to have everything figured out before reaching out.”

## 2:05–2:55 — Services
“Here are the three main service areas. I kept the descriptions short so the page remains easy to scan.

The sections cover anxiety and overwhelm, trauma and life transitions, and relationships and connection. Each one describes a real client need and then explains the type of support available.”

## 2:55–3:40 — Our Office
“I also added a completely new section called ‘Our Office,’ as requested in the assignment.

This section makes the physical practice space part of the experience. It explains that the office is calm, private and welcoming, and it also makes the in-person and online options clear.”

## 3:40–4:20 — FAQs and contact
“Next are the FAQs. I used an expandable layout so visitors can get answers without making the page extremely long.

At the bottom, the contact area is intentionally simple. Someone can leave their name, email and a short message. The goal is to make the first step feel easy rather than like a complicated intake form.”

## 4:20–4:50 — Mobile
“Now I’ll switch to the mobile view. The layout collapses into a single column, buttons remain easy to tap, text stays readable, and the imagery scales without breaking the flow.

The same core information remains available on mobile, but the page has been simplified for smaller screens.”

## 4:50–5:00 — Close
“That’s the first draft. The main goal was to make the website feel calm, trustworthy and human while keeping the original information architecture familiar. Thanks for reviewing it, and I’d be happy to refine the next round based on your feedback.”
